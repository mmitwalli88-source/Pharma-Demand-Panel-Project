DEMAND & FORECAST ACCURACY

The baseline fails where demand is planned to change

The three-month average carries a signed bias of -14.0% in planned-promotion months and +2.0% in the rest, so it under-forecasts exactly when stock is being pushed. Promoted months stock out in 12.8% of rows against 5.0%. A ridge regression on inputs known before the month begins cuts held-out error from 14.59% to 9.19%.

●
What the regression result does and does not show. It shows a one-month-ahead forecast built from inputs available before the month is more accurate than the moving average on these test rows. It does not show that adopting it would lower stockouts or cost: that needs a sequential replenishment simulation. The source publication quotes a test WAPE of 9.07%; the same experiment re-run here measures 9.19%, and this page reports the measured value.
The through-line. Forecast error is not spread evenly. It piles up where the future differs from the recent past: promotions and seasonal turns. That is where a better forecast, or a rule that adjusts the baseline for planned promotions, earns its place before any stock is added.
