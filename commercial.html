COMMERCIAL & INVENTORY CONTRIBUTION

What stock costs, and what unserved demand costs

Gross profit is EGP 32.97M at a 33.9% margin. Holding and expiry cost EGP 800k (2.4% of gross profit), while EGP 788k of demand went unserved and was never booked, 1.0 times the cost of carrying and expiring stock. That ratio is the price of a forecast error in each direction, and it is what a better forecast should be judged against.

From revenue to modelled contribution

Net inventory contribution is gross profit less holding and expiry cost. It is not net enterprise profit: staffing, transport, financing and tax are not in the file.

Gross margin by product family

Family, supplier and dosage form are one grouping here. Margin differs between families because price and purchase cost are set per product; with five products per family that is a catalogue difference, not a test result.

Revenue by sales channel

Channel is the dominant channel of each product-region series, and equals the customer segment one for one (Institutional = Clinic, Retail = Hospital, Wholesale = Pharmacy). Stockout share by channel is noise after accounting for 125 series (p = 0.12).

Where unserved demand sits, by promotion status

Lost revenue is potential selling value at net price, not recognised sales. Promoted months are 20.4% of volume and 41% of lost revenue.

Holding and expiry cost by family

Holding cost is 1.5% a month on average stock at purchase cost; expiry cost values removed packs at purchase cost. Batch-level shelf life is not in the file, so causes of expiry cannot be traced.

The through-line. Money moves in three places that are all small next to gross profit: stock that is held, stock that expires, and demand that is not met. None justifies a blanket policy change on its own, but together they set the price of a forecast error, and that price is what the forecast comparison should be judged against.
